title: Consumer Lag Testing with Khaos
description: Simulate Kafka consumer lag on purpose, both self-reported and real broker-side lag, to test alerting and backpressure handling with Khaos.
---

Consumer lag, the gap between what's been produced and what's been consumed, is usually
discovered in production, by an alert, after it's already a problem. Khaos lets you reproduce it
deliberately, on a cluster you control, so you can test how your consumers, dashboards, and alerts
actually behave before that happens.

<Terminal
  lines={[{ type: "command", text: "khaos run traffic/consumer-lag --lag-poll 5s" }]}
/>

## How lag actually grows

Lag grows whenever consumers can't keep up with producers. Khaos gives you two independent knobs to
force that:

- **Outpace the consumers.** Set `producer_rate` higher than your consumer topology can realistically
  sustain.
- **Slow the consumers down directly.** `consumer_delay_ms` adds artificial per-message processing
  time: the cleanest way to simulate "this consumer does real, slow work" without writing a slow
  consumer.

The bundled `traffic/consumer-lag` scenario combines both across two topics:

```yaml
topics:
  - name: payments
    producer_rate: 2000
    consumer_delay_ms: 50
    num_consumer_groups: 2
    consumers_per_group: 2

  - name: notifications
    producer_rate: 3000
    consumer_delay_ms: 100
    num_consumer_groups: 2
    consumers_per_group: 2
```

Under-provisioning `consumers_per_group` relative to a topic's `partitions` produces lag too, in a
more structural way than a per-message delay does. It's worth testing as a distinct case, since the fix
("add more consumers, up to the partition count") is different from the fix for slow processing.

## Self-reported lag vs. real broker lag

By default, the lag Khaos shows is self-reported: `Produced - Consumed` from its own in-process
counters. It's always available and has zero permission requirements, but it resets with the run
and can't account for anything outside Khaos touching the topic.

Pass `--lag-poll <interval>` to also poll the actual Kafka brokers for committed-offset lag, the
same number a real monitoring system would compute from consumer group offsets:

<Terminal
  lines={[{ type: "command", text: "khaos run traffic/consumer-lag --lag-poll 5s" }]}
/>

With `--lag-poll` on, the TUI shows both figures side by side. If your cluster denies
`DescribeGroups`/`OffsetFetch`/`ListOffsets` (common on some managed clusters), broker-side lag
reports as `unknown` rather than failing the run.

## What to actually test

- **Alert thresholds.** Does your paging rule fire at a sane lag value, and not flap on the normal
  lag blips of a healthy consumer catching up after a deploy?
- **Backpressure handling.** If a consumer applies backpressure or sheds load under lag, does that
  logic actually engage, and does it recover cleanly once producer rate or `consumer_delay_ms`
  returns to normal?
- **Dashboards.** Confirm a lag dashboard reflects broker-side truth, not just an application's own
  optimistic counters, by comparing what it shows against `--lag-poll`'s figure during the same run.
